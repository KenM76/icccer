Ken gives release authorisation in the shortest possible form — asked
whether to commit, push and tag, he replied **"all of it."** Take that as
authorisation for the complete sequence *including* the verification that
belongs with it (gates, changelog, version bump, watching the workflow),
not as three isolated commands.

**But surface anything irreversible-and-newly-discovered BEFORE doing it.**
On 2026-08-25, after he had already said "all of it", a subagent surfaced
that the tracked ledger carried material from a licensed corpus whose
publishing permission the project's own docs recorded as never granted.
The exposure was pre-existing and already public. It was raised as a
single question with three named options and a recommendation, and he
chose a middle path nobody had proposed — ship the release, and stop
adding any further material from that corpus.

**Why:** he engaged with the flag rather than being annoyed by it, and his
answer was better than either of the two obvious ones — so the flag paid
for itself. A blanket go-ahead covers the work *as understood when
given*; it does not cover an exposure discovered afterwards. Publishing
is the operator's act (project rule 9), and a licence question is flagged,
never decided alone.

**How to apply:** execute terse go-aheads in full and without re-asking
for each step. Interrupt exactly once, immediately before the first
irreversible action, and only when something material changed since the
go-ahead. Present it as: the fact, whether it is new or pre-existing,
what is already public, three concrete options, and a recommendation —
short. Then continue without waiting for further confirmation on the
parts already authorised.
